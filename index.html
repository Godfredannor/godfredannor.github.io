MSc coursework2024Acquisition, Retention and Conversion

Etsy: acquisition, retention and conversion plan

An audit of Etsy's website, social media, email and advertising, followed by a plan to attract more buyers, convert more visits into orders and keep customers coming back.

What I did

  • Audited the website with Website Grader and compared social engagement with follower numbers.
  • Reviewed email and community features, then ran PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces and SWOT analyses.
  • Set Serve, Sizzle and Save objectives from the 5Ss model and built an Online Value Proposition around convenience, choice and community.

What I found

  • Weak mobile optimisation and page speed, in a market where most shoppers use phones.
  • Low social engagement for the size of the following.
  • No email referral programme for existing customers.

What I recommended

  • Mobile-first design, image optimisation and A/B tests on layouts and checkout.
  • Influencer partnerships, shoppable social posts and seller stories.
  • Email referral and loyalty rewards, plus remarketing to abandoned carts.

MSc coursework2024Digital Marketing Strategy and Planning

Nike: digital marketing communications plan

An integrated plan to raise online engagement, retention and sales for Nike, built from a review of its website, app and social channels.

What I did

  • Analysed technology factors with PESTEL and evaluated the website, app and social channels in a SWOT.
  • Segmented the audience (18 to 35 primary, 35 to 50 secondary, across North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific) and set the positioning.
  • Wrote SMART objectives and a risk and evaluation plan.

What I found

  • A strong brand and social following.
  • Slow page loading that risks lost conversions.
  • A need for more personalised and interactive content to deepen engagement.

What I recommended

  • An integrated mix: social, content, email, SEO and website, paid search, influencer, mobile, video and interactive.
  • Email segmentation with automated sequences for onboarding, abandoned carts and post-purchase.
  • Sustainability messaging and monthly reviews with A/B testing.

MSc coursework2024Framework: SOSTAC

ABSA Bank Ghana: social media strategy

A social media audit and a four-week campaign plan for a Ghanaian bank, with sample creatives, a content calendar and a hypothetical budget.

What I did

  • Audited Facebook, X and Instagram, and compared the bank with GCB, Stanbic, Ecobank and MTN Mobile Money.
  • Ran SWOT and PESTEL analyses and set Sell, Serve, Speak, Save and Sizzle objectives.
  • Used the STOP model to define segments, targets and positioning, then drafted a four-week content plan.

What I found

  • Low engagement and an irregular posting schedule.
  • Content that leaned on product promotion, with little interactive or educational material.
  • Slow or missing replies to customer questions, and no distinct voice across channels.

What I recommended

  • A mix of financial education, customer stories and CSR content, posted at least three times a week per platform.
  • Prompt replies, polls, quizzes and user-generated content with branded hashtags.
  • Influencer and community partnerships, with live Q&A sessions and a webinar.

Sustainable practices, brand loyalty and purchase behaviour in UK fashion

Primary research into how sustainability practices influence loyalty and buying decisions among UK fashion consumers.

100UK fashion consumers, screened against set criteria (purposive sampling)
80%+of respondents viewed sustainability practices favourably for repeat purchases, recommendations and emotional connection
SPSS 25Google Forms survey, analysed with descriptive statistics

What I did

  • Designed and ran an online survey in Google Forms.
  • Analysed responses in IBM SPSS Statistics 25.
  • Framed results with the Theory of Planned Behaviour and the Stimuli-Organism-Response model.

What I found

  • Brand trust and perceived CSR emerged as key factors linking sustainability to loyalty.
  • Fair labour practices and sustainable packaging had the strongest influence on purchase decisions.
  • Respondents hesitated to pay premium prices for sustainable materials.

What I recommended

  • Loyalty programmes that reward sustainable choices, such as extra points for recycled items and clothing returns.
  • Transparent pricing that explains the cost of sustainable materials.
  • Educational content on durability and long-term value.

The sample was purposive, so the results describe these 100 respondents and not the whole UK market. Future work could test the relationships with inferential methods.
